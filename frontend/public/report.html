1. Executive Summary & Core Mission

1.1 The Operational Crisis in Retail & Store Decision-Making

Modern enterprise retail, pharmacy management, grocery commerce, and quick-delivery logistics operate under tight margins, fluctuating lead times, and intense demand volatility. Store owners and procurement officers face three critical challenges:

  • Intuition-Driven Procurement Errors: Store managers routinely reorder stock based on recent customer memory or subjective hunches rather than empirical sales velocity, lead-time variance, and holding carrying costs. This leads to severe stockouts during peak surges or massive cash lockup in dead inventory.
  • Gross vs. Net Profit Confusion: Standard accounting software itemizes gross margins on individual product sales but fails to incorporate daily store operating overhead (rent, electricity, refrigeration, staff wages, insurance) during order evaluation. Procurement that appears profitable on paper frequently generates net cash losses.
  • Disconnected & Static Spreadsheets: Store teams rely on fragile, disconnected spreadsheets that lack live catalog data, cannot model stochastic risks, ignore regulatory restrictions (such as Indian NPPA DPCO drug price ceilings or Schedule H prescription mandates), and cannot be shared securely across store branches.

1.2 The Mission & Architectural Mandate of DMOC

DMOC (Decision Marketing Operating System) unites rigorous decision science, real-world catalog and regulatory APIs, inventory mechanics, and reactive visual workflows into a unified platform. DMOC's primary objectives are:

  • Democratize Decision Science: Provides store managers and enterprise analysts with Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA), Expected Monetary Value (EMV), Expected Utility Theory, and 10,000-run Monte Carlo stochastic simulations.
  • Real-World Catalog & Regulatory Ingestion: Connects directly with Open Food Facts for grocery and FMCG barcode validation, and official NPPA / CDSCO registries for pharmaceutical maximum price ceilings and prescription compliance.
  • Visual Node-Based Decision Canvas: Allows non-technical operators to build non-linear computational flow graphs with 26 modular nodes, conditional if/else branching, and real-time execution tracing.
  • Transparent & Auditable Intelligence: Provides plain-language reasoning chains, interactive 3D spatial trees, break-even unit calculations, and printable executive briefs.
Core Philosophical Commitment
DMOC strictly distinguishes Gross Profit from Net Profit. Operating costs (store rent, electricity, wages, and logistics) are never assumed to be zero in any procurement or replenishment evaluation.

2. Theoretical Foundations & Mathematical Decision Science

DMOC is built upon established, peer-reviewed mathematical methodologies implemented in the core calculation engine (decisionEngine.ts). Every calculation is auditable, deterministic where required, and statistically robust.

2.1 Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)

Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis evaluates competing options across multiple orthogonal criteria, each assigned a relative percentage weight w_j such that the sum of all weights equals 100%:

∑ (from j=1 to m) w_j = 100%, where w_j > 0
Weight Normalization Constraint Across All Evaluation Dimensions

Linear Normalization

Criteria can represent either maximization (e.g., net revenue, shelf velocity, supplier reliability) or minimization (e.g., wholesale unit cost, return rate, working capital risk). DMOC maps disparate physical and financial units into dimensionless normalized utility values r_ij ∈ [0, 1]:

Maximization: r_ij = (x_ij - x_j^min) / (x_j^max - x_j^min) Minimization: r_ij = (x_j^max - x_ij) / (x_j^max - x_j^min)
Linear Utility Transformation (Handles zero-variance edge cases by assigning r_ij = 1.0)

Weighted Sum Model (WSM)

The composite performance score S_i for each competing alternative i is calculated as:

S_i = ∑ (from j=1 to m) w_j · r_ij
Aggregate Weighted Performance Score Determining Optimal Choice

2.2 Expected Monetary Value (EMV)

Under conditions of risk and discrete uncertainty, each alternative i produces a set of mutually exclusive potential outcomes O_ik, each associated with an empirical probability P(O_ik) and monetary payoff X_ik:

EMV_i = ∑ (from k=1 to K) P(O_ik) · X_ik, where ∑ P(O_ik) = 1.0
Expected Monetary Value Formula

2.3 Expected Utility Theory (von Neumann–Morgenstern)

In retail and inventory procurement, monetary value does not scale linearly with psychological satisfaction due to asymmetric downside risk aversion. A loss of $10,000 can cause bankruptcy, whereas a gain of $10,000 provides modest growth. DMOC maps monetary payoffs or qualitative outcomes to utility values U(O_ik) ∈ [0, 100]:

EU_i = ∑ (from k=1 to K) P(O_ik) · U(O_ik)
Risk-Averse Expected Utility Formulation

2.4 Monte Carlo Stochastic Risk Simulation

Real-world sales demand and supplier delivery windows are stochastic rather than fixed. DMOC executes a high-speed pseudo-random Monte Carlo engine running up to 10,000 iterations per scenario:

  • Probability Distribution Sampling: Draws random values across parameterized normal, uniform, or triangular distributions representing customer footfall and supplier fulfillment delays.
  • Empirical Percentile Estimation: Computes empirical percentiles including P10 (Pessimistic Downside with 90% confidence), P50 (Median Baseline), and P90 (Optimistic High-Demand Ceiling).
  • Statistical Moments: Calculates mean expected return μ, variance σ², standard deviation σ, and value-at-risk (VaR).

2.5 Sensitivity Analysis & Tipping Points

DMOC systematically perturbs individual criterion weights w_j from 0% to 100% while proportionately scaling remaining weights. It detects tipping points—the exact numerical weight threshold where the leading business recommendation flips to a competing alternative.

3. Financial & Inventory Mechanics

Store decisions must reflect financial reality. DMOC executes strict mathematical accounting across every inventory restock, pricing adjustment, and supplier order.

3.1 Gross Profit vs. Net Profit Breakdown

Traditional retail tools mislead store owners by displaying high gross margins while ignoring fixed store overhead. DMOC requires and calculates both:

Gross Revenue = Units Sold × Selling Price Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) = Units Sold × Purchase Price Gross Profit = Gross Revenue - COGS Gross Margin % = (Gross Profit / Gross Revenue) × 100
Gross Profit Formulations

To calculate true Net Profit, DMOC apportions the store's verified monthly operating overhead (rent, electricity, refrigeration, staff wages, insurance) proportionally across operational working days and sales volume:

Daily Operating Overhead = Monthly Operating Expenses / 30 Allocated Overhead = Daily Operating Overhead × Holding Window (Days) Net Profit = Gross Profit - Allocated Overhead - Holding/Financing Costs Net Margin % = (Net Profit / Gross Revenue) × 100
Net Bottom-Line Margin Formulations

3.2 Inventory Reorder Point (ROP) & Safety Stock

To prevent catastrophic stockouts during demand surges without tying up excess cash, DMOC utilizes dynamic safety stock and lead-time demand calculations:

Daily Sales Velocity (V_d) = (∑ Units Sold in Past N Days) / N Lead Time Demand = V_d × Supplier Lead Time (Days) Safety Stock = Z × σ_lead × √(Supplier Lead Time) Reorder Point (ROP) = Lead Time Demand + Safety Stock
Dynamic Reorder Point & Safety Stock Formulations (Z = 1.65 for 95% service level)

3.3 Working Capital Risk & Break-Even Quantity

Every purchase order commits working capital that cannot be deployed elsewhere. DMOC calculates the break-even volume and capital commitment horizon:

Capital Committed = Proposed Order Quantity × Unit Wholesale Cost Contribution Margin per Unit = Unit Selling Price - Unit Purchase Cost Break-Even Units = ⌈ Allocated Overhead / Contribution Margin per Unit ⌉ Days of Inventory Remaining = Current Stock on Hand / V_d
Working Capital & Break-Even Formulations

4.3 Architectural Execution Pipeline

1. Ingestion: Client queries are parsed by the Natural Language Processor, extracting business context, product entities, proposed volumes, and time horizons.
2. Enrichment: The backend checks local store inventory. If unlisted or needing verification, it queries Open Food Facts or CDSCO registries.
3. Multi-Path Simulation: The Inventory Decision Engine models Path A (Proposed), Path B (Status Quo), and Path C (Calibrated Optimal).
4. Visual Graph Compilation: The simulation payload is automatically compiled into an editable directed acyclic graph (DAG) on the workflow canvas.
5. Execution & Audit: Running the workflow evaluates topological dependencies, recording execution times and node outputs into an immutable audit ledger.

5. Data Architecture & Schema Reference

DMOC models real-world store operations and decision science using an enterprise Prisma schema supporting multi-tenant workspace isolation and transactional integrity.

5.1 Multi-Store Business & Catalog Models

  • Business Profile: Represents a distinct store, shop, or enterprise workspace. Contains monthly revenue, fixed operating costs, currency code (INR, USD, EUR), tax percentage, and the critical isSample boolean flag.
  • Product Catalog: Itemizes physical stock, SKU/barcode, category (Medicine, FMCG, Grocery), purchase cost, selling price, reorder threshold, shelf life in days, prescription requirements (Schedule H/H1), and legal price ceiling (DPCO MRP).
  • Supplier Ledger: Tracks wholesale vendors, contact records, payment terms (Net 30, COD), lead time in days, minimum order quantity (MOQ), and reliability scoring.
  • Stock Movements: Append-only audit trail capturing every inventory increment, customer sale, stock write-off, or return, ensuring tamper-proof stock reconciliations.
  • Sales Ledger: Records timestamped sales transactions with unit price, discount, tax, and calculated net profit contribution.

5.2 Decision & Workflow Graph Models

  • Decision: Stores the strategic decision inquiry, category (business, retail, pharmacy, financial), description, status, and deadline.
  • Alternative: Competing choices being evaluated (e.g., 'Order 50 Packs', 'Wait 1 Week', 'Order 25 Packs').
  • Criterion: Evaluation dimensions (e.g., 'Net Profit Margin', 'Stockout Risk', 'Holding Cost') with maximization/minimization directions and priority weights.
  • Outcome: Quantifiable consequences attached to alternatives with probability, monetary payoff, and risk tier (low, medium, high, critical).
  • Workflow: Defines visual flow graphs with versioning, auto-increment counters, and template references.
  • WorkflowNode: Individual blocks on the canvas with coordinates (positionX, positionY), node type (26 types), and serialized JSON configuration.
  • WorkflowEdge: Directed connections linking a sourceNodeId and sourceHandle to a targetNodeId and targetHandle.
  • WorkflowRun: Execution logs capturing run timestamp, overall status (running, completed, failed), and node-by-node execution duration in milliseconds.
Data Integrity & Isolation Guarantee
DMOC workspaces explicitly separate sample demonstration stores (such as 'Arogya Medical Store' and 'FreshMart Grocery') from real user businesses using the isSample: true database flag. Sample data is clearly badged in the UI and can be deleted or reset with a single click without affecting real business ledgers.

6. Natural Language Decision Wizard & Real Data Ingestion

DMOC features an intelligent decision wizard (BusinessDecisionWizard.tsx) that allows non-technical store operators to submit business dilemmas in everyday plain English.

6.1 The Natural Language Processing Pipeline

When an operator types: "I own a pharmacy store. Should I order 50 additional packs of Paracetamol 650mg before the weekend?"

  • Step 1 (Context Detection): Detects that the user operates a medical store or pharmacy.
  • Step 2 (Entity Extraction): Extracts 'Paracetamol 650mg' as target product and '50 units' as requested replenishment.
  • Step 3 (Temporal Horizon): Identifies 'weekend' as a 3-day high-demand sales velocity window.
  • Step 4 (Catalog Correlation): Scans active workspace inventory. If found, loads current stock and supplier terms; if missing, initiates real-world API lookup.

6.2 External Data Verification & Regulatory Compliance

To eliminate guessing, DMOC cross-references queries against official external APIs:

  • Open Food Facts API: Real-time lookup across millions of grocery and FMCG products. Retrieves barcode data, brand verification, pack sizes, ingredients, and categories.
  • NPPA Drug Price Ceilings: Verifies medicine prices against statutory National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) DPCO ceiling price caps. Prevents store owners from configuring illegal selling prices exceeding legal maximums.
  • CDSCO Prescription Regulations: Automatically tags Schedule H and H1 prescription drugs, alerting store operators that audited medical prescription records are legally required.

7. The Visual Node-Based Decision Workflow Canvas

DMOC features a visual computational flow engine built on @xyflow/react. It allows users to wire complex, multi-variable logic using 26 modular nodes.

7.1 Graph Execution & Topological Dependency Sorting

  • Topological Dependency Sorting: The engine executes a Depth-First Search (DFS) topological sort of the Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) before running, ensuring that parent output handles are resolved before dependent child nodes execute.
  • Cycle Detection: Detects circular loops and notifies the user with clear node IDs, preventing infinite execution deadlocks.
  • Dynamic Branch Pruning: When an if_else node evaluates to true, child nodes on the true handle execute; downstream nodes on the false branch are marked as skipped in the execution drawer.

8. Store Operations & Decision Suite

DMOC bridges high-level decision theory with everyday shopfloor operations, providing live inventory visibility, supplier terms, and spatial 3D visualization.

8.1 Inventory & Catalog Operations

  • Batch & Expiry Monitoring: Tracks batches and shelf-life horizons. Perishable grocery and pharmaceutical items expiring within 30 days display prominent yellow warning badges; items expiring within 7 days trigger red markdown alerts.
  • Stock Level Tracking: Tracks real-time stock on hand against minimum reorder points. Highlights cash tied up in dead stock.
  • Stock Movement Audit: Complete ledger recording stock receipts, customer sales, supplier returns, and write-offs.

8.2 Supplier Management & Terms

  • Vendor Directory: Records vendor name, contact person, phone, email, and catalog mappings.
  • Lead-Time Tracking: Incorporates supplier lead times (in days) into dynamic reorder point calculations.
  • Commercial Terms: Models payment terms (Net 30, Net 60, Cash on Delivery) to project cash outflow schedules.
  • MOQ & Volume Tiers: Evaluates supplier minimum order quantities (MOQ) and volume discounts during restock evaluations.

8.3 Interactive 3D Decision Tree & 2D Fallback

DMOC provides dual visualization interfaces for inspecting complex decision hierarchies:

  • Interactive 3D Spatial Tree: Constructed using Three.js and React Three Fiber. Visualizes the decision problem as a spatial node-and-link tree with orbital camera controls, mousewheel zooming, color-coded risk paths, and click-to-inspect modals.
  • Resilient 2D Canvas Fallback: Engineered in pure SVG for ultra-low latency on mobile devices or environments where WebGL is unavailable or disabled.
Visualization Resilience Guarantee
DMOC's 3D and 2D decision tree engines render the exact same underlying mathematical tree hierarchy. Switching between modes preserves selected node states and calculation outputs instantly.

9.2 Security & Authentication Protocols

  • Argon2id Password Encryption: All passwords are salted and hashed using Argon2id with 64MB memory cost, 3 iterations, and 4 threads, providing high resistance against GPU and ASIC brute-force attacks.
  • Dual-Token Rotation: Dual-token system using short-lived JWT access tokens (15-minute expiration) and cryptographically signed refresh tokens stored in secure, SameSite=Lax, HttpOnly cookies.
  • Immutable Audit Logging: Every workspace update, inventory write-off, and workflow run creates an append-only entry in the AuditEvent database table.

10. End-to-End User Operating Manual & Deployment

10.1 Step-by-Step Walkthrough Guide

  • Step 1: Onboarding Your Store: Visit the DMOC homepage and click 'Get Started'. Complete the 4-step onboarding modal: (1) Business Name & Type, (2) Currency & Tax Rate, (3) Monthly Turnover & Operating Costs, and (4) Optional Sample Data Seeding.
  • Step 2: Switching Store Workspaces: Use the Workspace Selector dropdown in the top sidebar to toggle between different store locations or demo profiles seamlessly.
  • Step 3: Running Natural Language Decisions: Navigate to the 'Decision Wizard'. Enter your dilemma in everyday language (e.g., 'Should I order 40 units of Almond Milk before Friday?'). Click 'Analyze Business Decision'.
  • Step 4: Evaluating the 3-Path Model: Compare Path A (Full Order), Path B (Do Nothing), and Path C (Calibrated Optimal). Inspect capital committed, stockout probability, and true net profit.
  • Step 5: Visual Workflow Customization: Click 'Convert to Visual Workflow'. Drag additional logic nodes (e.g., 'If/Else' or 'Threshold Check') onto the canvas. Click 'Run Flow' to execute real-time DAG simulation.
  • Step 6: Generating Executive Briefs & Reports: Visit the 'Reports' page to review the DMOC Executive Brief. Click 'Print / Save as PDF' for executive physical documentation, or download complete Excel data books from Settings.

10.3 Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

  • Q: Can DMOC operate without an active internet connection?
    A: Yes. DMOC functions fully on local SQLite or offline PostgreSQL. External API queries queue automatically until connectivity is restored.
  • Q: Does DMOC monetize or share private sales figures?
    A: Never. DMOC enforces strict tenant isolation. All business turnover, margins, and catalog records belong exclusively to your instance and are never shared.
  • Q: How does DMOC prevent stockouts during sudden demand surges?
    A: DMOC integrates seasonal velocity multipliers, lead-time variance buffers, and safety stock calculations to increase reorder points ahead of anticipated trading rushes.
